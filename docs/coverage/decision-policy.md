# From scores to recommendations

A score estimates performance on the registered benchmark panel at one model revision, effort and execution setting. The observed rows identify that estimate. Provider reputation, release order and assumed model size do not enter the calculation. If a small model wins on shared tasks, the score should reflect it. If its lead depends on a single specialist test, the reader needs to see that too.

## When the index names a winner

For Coding, Writing, Design and Research v3 and the software-engineering stack index v2, ProChoice keeps point estimates on the price frontier and makes endorsement a separate decision. Lowest cost identifies the lowest estimated task cost in the displayed selection. A general winner or value recommendation must satisfy these editorial evidence requirements:

- At least three measured benchmarks for Coding and Research, or two for Writing, Design and the stack index.
- At least two collection feeds and two task families. For example, EQ Longform and WritingBench Academic & Engineering cover distinct writing tasks from different producers, but both arrive through the writing-feeds adapter and count as one collection feed. The stack index distinguishes terminal work, repository changes and repository comprehension.
- At least one quarter of the calibrated panel's weight directly observed. The feed, count and weight thresholds define what ProChoice is willing to recommend; they are policy choices.
- The recommendation must survive the missing-score sensitivity check. For the overall winner, the score difference against **every** eligible alternative in the selection must exceed or equal 1.96 standard errors, with a strictly positive weighted difference on their shared benchmarks. Without a shared result, the comparison rests entirely on the statistical link.
- A value pick must clear the same comparison against the budget configuration and have at least 50% estimated frontier membership on its provider curve. The value rule then considers the score gained for the additional spend. This is a tradeoff for the stated price axis. A user's subscription or capacity limits can change the choice.

The highest numerical estimate can therefore appear without a winner endorsement. The domain page lists unresolved alternatives and explains which comparisons rely entirely on links. An unresolved comparison is insufficient evidence of a difference; it does not establish equivalent performance. Individual score intervals use the empirical-Bayes mean plus or minus 1.96 posterior standard errors. These approximate marginal intervals are conditional on the registered panel and the uncertainty model. The model treats task sampling errors on distinct benchmarks as independent and extrapolates from observed to missing task families. A positive correlation on a small linking panel gives limited evidence about that extrapolation. The pairwise rule does not supply simultaneous 95% coverage or an anytime-valid inference across daily updates.

## What the validation establishes

A large evaluation suite can repeatedly measure the same skill. ProChoice assigns task families before ranking models, preserves the exact benchmark edition in each epoch, and reports how much the results depend on the weighting choice. The held-out audit removes a whole feed, refits calibration without its observations, and compares the predicted order with the omitted results on configurations both methods can score. Different training exposure, common judges, and selective reporting can still affect both sides. The matched-cohort comparison showed no consistent predictive advantage for empirical quality weights across the four domains. [Family-weight study](measurement-families.md) · [Rank evidence and baselines](rank-evidence.md)

Three empirical findings informed this distinction. Alzahrani and colleagues found ranking changes after small changes to multiple-choice protocols, which argues for recording the evaluation setting. The AI Cartography study found local dependence among benchmarks in an existing leaderboard, which motivates examining shared measurement families. Neither study selects ProChoice's families or cutoff values. Statistical work on AI evaluation also distinguishes uncertainty conditional on a fixed benchmark from generalization to new items; claims about a user's future work require evidence beyond the published scores.
