# Coding and research coverage

Reviewed November 21, 2026. Coding compares models under the evaluator's prescribed setup. Research covers finding, interpreting and synthesizing evidence, quantitative analysis and scientific reasoning. A model's research score alone cannot tell a buyer how well it checks citations. The open-web benchmarks below illustrate where direct evidence is still thin.

## Changes to the indices

| Decision | Evidence and effect |
| --- | --- |
| Include ITBench-AA in Coding | A model investigates an offline snapshot of a Kubernetes incident. It must identify every contributing entity, and false positives reduce its score. Stirrup gives each model the same tools. The evaluation contains 59 incidents, including 19 private incidents. Three runs of one incident contribute one task to our uncertainty calculation. The [May 27 release](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/itbench-aa-launch) established the public and private task split. The public evaluation page and model table together yielded 32 distinct score rows, 28 with recognized configurations across eight providers at review. [Evaluator and methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/itbench-aa) |
| Correct LiveBench Coding uncertainty | The November 6 release has 24 bug fixes. The seven difficulty and subject groups in its breakdown sum to 24, and every model's cost row lists 24 coding tasks. The old denominator of 120 understated uncertainty. [Release breakdown](https://livebench.ai/data/20261106/breakdown.json) · [Per-model counts](https://livebench.ai/data/20261106/cost.csv) |
| Retire the AA SciCode series from Coding at the epoch boundary | Artificial Analysis marks this evaluation as legacy. The November 21 v1 audit recorded saturation pressure of 0.90 and zero separable pairs among the nine leading provider configurations. The underlying scientific programming task remains relevant, and the original results remain in the v1 archive. [AA evaluation](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/scicode) · [Coding v1 archive](../../data/epochs/coding-v1.0.0.json) |
| Include Harvey LAB-AA v1.1 in Research | Models produce a legal deliverable from case documents. The score measures whether the work meets every task criterion and avoids material hallucinations. The 120 private tasks cover 24 practice areas. Three judges score the same deliverable. The [October 8 release](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/harvey-lab-aa-v1-1) changed both the headline metric and the judges. The first-party board and model table together yielded 45 distinct rows, 41 with recognized configurations across nine providers. The legal-work score does not isolate prose style. [Evaluator and methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/harvey-lab-aa) |
| Correct the scope of three research measures | MLCR measures reasoning over fragmented medical records. MKA measures multimodal professional knowledge work across ten industries using the weighted satisfaction of difficult criteria. VIALS tests scientific image interpretation and reports the share of 161 tasks answered correctly three times. Both MKA and VIALS run in Stirrup. [MLCR](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mlcr-aa) · [MKA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mka) · [VIALS](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/vials-aa) |
| Correct AA-AnalystAgent uncertainty | The evaluator asks 80 spreadsheet and document questions. A question passes only if the model answers correctly on all five attempts. The previous registry used 100 questions. [Evaluator and methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/aa-analyst-agent) |
| Remove ARC-AGI-2 from Research | The task asks for a transformation of a grid given input-output demonstrations. It provides direct evidence of abstract reasoning, with no requirement to find sources, analyze a dataset or synthesize a report. Historical Research v1 scores retain that contribution. [Epoch evaluation](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/arc-agi-2) · [Research v1 archive](../../data/epochs/research-v1.0.0.json) |

Five ITBench scores came from the general leaderboard. Its catalog identifies the same edition, and all shared score-bearing model IDs agreed with the detailed board. Each summary-only observation links to that leaderboard. Conflicting scores and model identities are quarantined ahead of degraded-cache retention.

The additional Chartography and RiemannBench series measure chart interpretation and mathematical problem solving. The release-card review records the provenance of those scores and the models for which they are available. [Model-card review](model-cards.md)

## Coding source decisions

| Work a user needs done | Evidence retained or screened |
| --- | --- |
| Repair and extend a repository | FrontierCode-AA 2.0 tests maintainer acceptance on 132 private tasks. DeepSWE v1.1 and SWE-bench Pro Public v2 supply independent repository-level tasks. Only the registered Pro split and scaffold enter the index. OpenAI's audit found flawed tests on at least 59.4% of its failure-enriched sample of 138 SWE-bench Verified problems and signs of training exposure. Reintroducing Verified alongside Pro would weaken this comparison. [FrontierCode](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/frontiercode-2-0) · [DeepSWE](https://deepswe.datacurve.ai/) · [Pro v2](https://scale.com/leaderboard/swe_bench_pro_public_v2) · [Pro paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941) · [OpenAI audit](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) |
| Build, debug and optimize systems | Terminal-Bench 4.0 measures terminal work. ITBench adds root-cause diagnosis. MirrorCode asks for implementations from behavioral examples. ALE-Bench measures contest optimization, and FrontierSWE includes performance and scientific computing. The table uses the component scores once, with each source's own units. [Terminal-Bench 4.0](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/terminalbench-4-0) · [MirrorCode](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/mirrorcode) · [ALE-Bench](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/ale-bench) · [FrontierSWE](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontierswe) |
| Work with recent tasks and unfamiliar code | LiveBench supplies a fixed November release. The SWE-rebench board also offers a 111-problem May–July window. For that whole window, only Alibaba and DeepSeek have allowlisted model rows with release dates preceding May 15. Zhipu's listed GLM release was May 16. Several frontier rows therefore cover problems already public at model release. A new cohort with five identifiable labs on the same post-release tasks would qualify for a fresh review. [LiveBench release notes](https://livebench.ai/data/20261106/notes.json) · [SWE-rebench board](https://swe-rebench.com/) · [SWE-rebench v2 dataset](https://huggingface.co/collections/nebius/swe-rebench-v2) |
| Compare product agents | Android Bench 2.0 publishes effort, confidence intervals and cost for 30 long-horizon tasks, but changes agent with provider. The OpenHands GSO rows include unknown effort for several frontier models. CursorBench also measures a product setup. These scores cannot fill raw-model cells with the same name. [Android Bench](https://developer.android.com/bench) · [GSO](https://gso-bench.github.io/) · [CursorBench](https://cursor.com/cursorbench) |
| Solve contest and freelance tasks | AA marks its LiveCodeBench series legacy, and its highest published result exceeds 91%. Epoch's Aider Polyglot export has no model release after December 2025. SWE-Lancer combines freelance implementation and choosing a technical proposal. Its July 2025 update changed execution conditions. The public split and launch results did not establish a current five-provider comparison under a common protocol. ## Research source decisions

| Research activity | Evidence retained or screened |
| --- | --- |
| Find and synthesize evidence | The 22 LiveBench research briefs require written reports on regulatory, legal, financial, strategy or science questions. MLCR and the document arena add long-context evidence. FutureSearch's distinct Deep Research Bench supplies 169 frozen-web tasks. At review its first-party page contained 37 model configurations from four providers. Only three providers had rows covering all 169 unique instances. A working source feed alone cannot meet the five-provider calibration rule. [LiveBench breakdown](https://livebench.ai/data/20261106/breakdown.json) · [FutureSearch DRB](https://evals.futuresearch.ai/#drb) |
| Browse the open web | BrowseComp's original table includes both closed-book models and a trained deep research agent. BrowseComp-Plus measures how much the retriever changes the result. GPT-5 achieved 55.9% with BM25 and 70.1% with Qwen3-Embedding-8B. DeepSearchQA's public data contains 900 prompts, answers and categories across 17 fields; that file contains no model-run records. Google specifies a judge and prompt. Scores from model cards with different search tools or graders cannot be pooled into one experiment. [BrowseComp](https://openai.com/index/browsecomp/) · [BrowseComp-Plus, ACL 2026](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1023) · [DeepSearchQA](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/deepsearchqa) · [Google's benchmark](https://www.kaggle.com/benchmarks/google/dsqa) |
| Produce a cited report | The DeepResearch Bench II producer publishes model, organization, valid task count and scores for whole products. Its CSV includes systems with different harnesses and task counts. DRACO measures report quality with expert rubrics and includes citation quality, but its public task dataset does not identify a common base-model protocol. Neither headline can be assigned to a generic chat-model effort setting. [DRB II board data](https://huggingface.co/spaces/muset-ai/DeepResearch-Bench-Leaderboard/blob/main/data_drb2/leaderboard.csv) · [DRACO dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/perplexity-ai/draco) |
| Analyze data and produce professional work | LiveBench Data Analysis supplies 150 short table tasks. AnalystAgent and Harvey LAB add spreadsheets and case documents. APEX-Agents covers long-horizon professional services. AA-Briefcase and GDPval-AA score mixed work products, including slides and spreadsheets; both stay in Research. GDPval-AA is a legacy source but still separated 82% of top-provider pairs in the November 21 v1 audit. [LiveBench](https://livebench.ai/data/20261106/notes.json) · [APEX-Agents](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/apex-agents) · [Briefcase](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/aa-briefcase) · [GDPval-AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa) |
| Check facts and reason through scientific problems | SimpleQA Verified measures closed-book factual accuracy. Omniscience, HLE-Diamond, VIALS, CritPt, Terminal-Bench-Science, FrontierMath and ProofBench give evidence on recall, visual interpretation, scientific workflows and mathematical reasoning. None directly measures the reliability of a cited research report. PaperBench would add genuine research replication. Its board combines BasicAgent and IterativeAgent, changes the time budget, and covers fewer than five labs on the full evaluation. Its 8,316 rubric items belong to only 20 papers. [SimpleQA](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/simple-qa-verified) · [Omniscience](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/omniscience) · [Terminal-Bench-Science](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/terminal-bench-science) · [PaperBench board](https://github.com/openai/preparedness/tree/main/project/paperbench) |
| Predict future events | ForecastBench includes web-enabled and closed-book systems with different numbers of predictions. FutureSearch's BTF-3 mirror has 15 rows, two agent setups and four organizations. Neither reviewed table supplies five eligible labs running one comparable protocol. [ForecastBench](https://www.forecastbench.org/) · [FutureSearch BTF-3](https://evals.futuresearch.ai/#btf3) · [Epoch export](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip) |

The remaining gaps include citation accuracy for current base models, open-web retrieval under common tooling, independent paper replication, mobile development under a fixed agent, and fine-grained competitive programming on problems published after training. Revisit these decisions when a producer publishes identifiable runs for the missing configurations. Record the score scale and the available evidence on uncertainty. When the evaluator omits both the denominator and uncertainty, disclose that limitation.

In Epoch’s published Tier 4 v2 export, GPT-6 Astra medium, high, xhigh and max each round to 97.6%. The unrounded medium score is 0.9756097561 and the other three are 0.976. The four rows have distinct source IDs and explicit effort suffixes, share one start timestamp and omit run logs. ProChoice preserves the reported measurements. The export provides no trial-level evidence to distinguish these four efforts. [Epoch data archive, frontiermath_tier_4_v2.csv](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip)
